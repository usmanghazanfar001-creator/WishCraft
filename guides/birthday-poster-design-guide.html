Birthday Poster Design Guide: Layout, Color & Text Tips

By WishCraft · Updated October 23, 2026

A birthday poster is a still image you can post, print or send. A good one is simple: one strong photo, a clear name, a short message and a color scheme that matches the person. This guide explains the choices that make the biggest difference, whether you use our poster generator or any other design tool.

Know where it will be used

WishCraft posters are portrait images at 1080 × 1350 pixels (a 4:5 ratio). That size is ideal for Instagram and Facebook posts, WhatsApp status and most phone screens. If you want to print, it works well at small sizes such as A5, but for large prints use a dedicated design program with higher resolution.

Layout: keep one focal point

The eye should land in three places, in order: the photo, the name, the message. Anything else is decoration. Practical rules:

  • Leave breathing room around the photo and text. Crowded posters look cheap.
  • Keep the name the largest piece of text after the heading.
  • Center-align for formal and elegant posters, and left-align for modern or casual styles.
  • Don't place important text near the very edges, because social apps crop and overlay UI.

Choosing the photo

  • Use a high-resolution portrait where the face fills a good portion of the frame.
  • Check the lighting: even, natural light beats harsh flash.
  • Use the zoom control to center the face; for circles, keep the eyes near the middle.
  • Pick a photo shape to match the mood: circle for friendly, rounded for warm, square for modern.

Color: match the person and the mood

Contrast matters more than color choice: light text on a dark background or dark text on a light one. If you must squint, change it.

Typography

WishCraft offers an elegant serif, a clean sans-serif and a playful monospace. A few guidelines apply anywhere:

  • Use no more than two fonts on a poster.
  • Serif fonts feel traditional and warm. Sans-serif fonts feel modern and friendly.
  • Avoid all-caps for long sentences; save it for the heading and name.
  • Keep text large enough to read on a phone at arm's length.

Message length

A poster is not a letter. The message box accepts up to 220 characters, but shorter is almost always better: two sentences read comfortably, five feel crowded. Choose from preset styles such as default, professional, friendly, heartfelt or short, then edit the text to sound like you. For ideas see our birthday wish collection.

Effects, video and GIF

You can add effects such as balloons, confetti, sparkles or hearts. For export you have three options:

  • Image: the best choice for printing, posting and sending.
  • Video: a short looping clip (MP4 where supported, WebM otherwise) ideal for status and stories.
  • GIF: a 480-pixel-wide looping animation that works in nearly any chat app.

Use effects with restraint. A subtle effect enhances a good design, but a heavy one can hide the photo and the text.

Pre-flight checklist

  1. Is the name spelled correctly?
  2. Is the age right (or deliberately left off)?
  3. Can you read everything at phone size?
  4. Is the photo sharp and the face centered?
  5. Does the sender line say who it is from?
