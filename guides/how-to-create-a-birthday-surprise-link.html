How to Create a Birthday Surprise Link With Photos and Music

By WishCraft · Updated October 23, 2026

A birthday surprise link is a single web address that opens a short animated experience for the person you are celebrating: a gift box, a cake ceremony, a story built from your photos, a message in your words and, if you like, music. It works on any phone with a browser, so there is nothing for the recipient to install.

This tutorial walks through each step of the WishCraft wish creator and explains what each option does, so the final result feels intentional rather than assembled.

What you will need
  • The birthday person's name (the only required field)
  • One clear photo of them, ideally a portrait
  • Optional: up to 10 more photos, a song, and a message in your own words
  • About ten minutes

Step 1: Enter the birthday details

Type the name exactly as you want it to appear on the reveal screen. You can add their age and your relationship to them; the age is used for the optional age-counter scene. Two toggles are worth knowing about:

  • Surprise Mode hides the name until the recipient opens the wish, which keeps the preview screen mysterious.
  • Gift box opening adds an animated gift they tap to open before the main experience begins.

Step 2: Add the main photo

This is the photo shown on the hero screen. Choose a sharp, well-lit picture where the face is clearly visible. WishCraft compresses the photo automatically so it loads quickly on mobile data. You can pick a circle, rounded, square or soft-frame shape, and one of eleven frame styles. If you are unsure, start with the circle and a simple gold frame; it suits almost every photo. For more guidance see our photo selection tips.

Step 3: Build the memory story

Add up to ten photos to create a short slideshow that plays before the reveal. Pick a transition (fade, Ken Burns zoom, slide or cinematic) and a duration per photo from three to ten seconds. Five seconds is a good default; shorter feels rushed and longer loses attention. A preview in the editor lets you flip through the photos before you commit.

Step 4: Write the message

The message box holds up to 260 characters, which is intentionally short so it stays readable on a phone screen. You can start from a quick style (heartfelt, funny, short, formal, blessing or Urdu) and edit it. Add a heading such as "To the best sister in the world" and sign with your name in the From field. Our guide to writing a heartfelt message shows how to fit a lot of feeling into a few lines.

Step 5: Add music

Upload a song (up to 25 MB) and drag the start and end sliders to choose the exact part that plays, for example the chorus. If you don't upload a song, you can switch on the music-box "Happy Birthday" melody. Only use music you have the right to share; see our terms of use for details.

Step 6: Pick a theme and animations

There are 22 themes. Choose one that matches the person: a warm gold for a parent, a bold color for a teenager, a soft pastel for a sibling. Then select animations such as confetti, balloons, sparkles, fireworks, hearts, golden particles, petals, bubbles or shooting stars, and set the intensity. Medium is best for most devices; choose low if the recipient may use an older phone.

Step 7: Try the bonus scenes

All bonus scenes are optional and off by default:

  • Blow the candles replaces the cake-cutting scene with a candle ceremony.
  • Countdown adds a 3-2-1 before the reveal.
  • Age counter counts up to the person's age.
  • Reasons you're special shows up to six short lines as cards.
  • Scratch card hides a message or gift idea under a surface they scratch away.
  • Lock until a date and time shows a live countdown if they open the link early, so you can send it ahead of the day.

Step 8: Preview, then generate the link

Always press Preview Birthday Experience and watch it from start to finish, ideally on your phone. Check that the name is spelled correctly, the music starts where you want and the photos are in the right order. When you are happy, press Create Wish & Generate Link. Your photos, song and text are saved and you receive a short link that looks like wishcrafting.vercel.app/?w=Ab3xK9pQzM.

Step 9: Share it

Use the WhatsApp button, the native Share sheet or copy the link. When the recipient finishes, they can watch again, send you a thank-you message or create a wish of their own. Read how to share birthday wishes on WhatsApp for timing and etiquette tips.

Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to try
Photo won't uploadUse a JPG, PNG or WebP file. Very large photos are resized automatically, but a poor connection can still interrupt the upload; try again on Wi-Fi.
Song is too largeThe limit is 25 MB. Trim the file or export it at a lower bitrate first.
No sound for the recipientPhones often block autoplay until the screen is tapped. The wish starts with an "Open" button for that reason, so the music begins after their first tap.
Link says wish not foundCheck that the whole link was copied, including the part after ?w=.
Lost your half-finished wishDrafts auto-save on the device you were using. Reopen the creator on the same browser and use the draft bar at the top.
