How to Create a Birthday Video With Photos and Music

By WishCraft · Updated November 8, 2026

A birthday video made from real photos and a song the person loves is one of the most replayed gifts you can send. It doesn't take editing skills. It takes a plan: a clear story, the right photos in the right order, music that fits and a format that suits where you'll share it.

This guide covers the whole process, whichever app you use, and then shows exactly what you can do with WishCraft, including the limits, so you don't waste time on something it can't do.

Step 1: Decide what kind of video you are making

TypeLengthBest for
Quick status video5 to 15 secondsWhatsApp status, Instagram story, a fast greeting
Photo slideshow with music30 to 90 secondsA personal surprise for a partner, parent or best friend
Friends-and-family montage1 to 3 minutesMilestone birthdays, with short clips from several people

Choose before you open any app. A 15-second status video and a 90-second slideshow need very different amounts of material.

Step 2: Collect and choose the photos

Pick six to ten photos for a slideshow, fewer for a status video. Prefer sharp, well-lit pictures with faces, mix close-ups with wider scenes and avoid near-duplicates. Our guide to choosing photos for a memory story has a full checklist.

Step 3: Put the photos in a story order

  1. Start with a hook: a photo that makes them smile straight away.
  2. Build the middle: chronological order is the safest, from the oldest memory to the newest.
  3. End strong: finish on the happiest or most recent photo, so the last second lands.

Step 4: Choose the music

  • Pick a song with meaning for the two of you, not just a popular one.
  • Use the chorus or the most emotional 30 to 60 seconds rather than the whole track.
  • Make sure the pace fits: slow songs suit longer photo durations, upbeat songs suit faster cuts.
  • Use only music you have the right to share. Platforms may mute or remove videos with copyrighted tracks.

Step 5: Set the timing

Five seconds per photo is comfortable for most slideshows. Use three seconds for upbeat music and many photos, and seven to ten when you want people to linger. Keep the total under about 90 seconds unless it's a milestone tribute.

Step 6: Add a message and preview

A short written message, two or three sentences, gives the video a heart. Then watch the whole thing on the device the recipient will use, with sound on. Check names, spelling and where the music starts and ends.

Sharing the finished video

  • For WhatsApp, a video under about 16 MB sends without heavy compression. A link has no size problem at all.
  • For stories and status, use a vertical format.
  • Send it at a time that suits the recipient's time zone. See how to share birthday wishes on WhatsApp.

Common mistakes

  • Too many photos, so the story drags.
  • Music that drowns out the mood, or starts at a quiet intro.
  • Low-resolution photos forwarded through chat apps.
  • Not previewing with sound on.
